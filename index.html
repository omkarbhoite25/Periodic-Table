About & methodology

This is a reference table for people planning or studying space missions. Every element carries the same five views, chosen with the switcher at the top.

Cosmic abundance shows solar-system abundance as log10 atoms per 106 silicon atoms, after Lodders (2003, 2021), to about ±0.1 dex. Elements with no reliable tabulated solar-system abundance are hatched: the synthetic elements and the short-lived natural traces.

Where to find it colors each element by its most accessible known off-Earth source. The detail panel lists every source with a confidence word: measured means detected in returned samples or by instruments at the body; inferred means deduced indirectly. M-type asteroid compositions are inferred from iron meteorites; no M-type has been sampled. Each pathway carries a maturity label: demonstrated in flight, lab or ground test, concept, or ship from Earth.

Mission role colors each element by its primary role aboard spacecraft or habitats; corner dots mark additional roles. Uses are labeled flown, flown, retired, ground test, planned, in development or speculative. Flown means demonstrated in space. Ground test means demonstrated only on Earth. Planned or in development means funded or designed but not flown. Speculative means not demonstrated and not committed; treat it as an idea, not a capability.

Origin shows the dominant nucleosynthesis source after Johnson (2019), with a secondary source where one contributes substantially. Polonium to actinium, protactinium, neptunium and plutonium are hatched: their natural occurrence is limited to trace amounts, from uranium and thorium decay, neutron capture on uranium, or primordial Pu-244. Technetium and promethium also occur only as natural traces but keep their source color.

Supply risk is an editorial heuristic for comparison only, not an official criticality assessment. Score = Earth criticality (0–3, higher means scarcer or more concentrated supply) + (3 − off-world availability, 0–3). Buckets: Low 0–1, Moderate 2–3, High 4, Critical 5–6. Technetium, promethium, astatine, radon, francium, radium, actinium, protactinium and all elements from curium (Z 96) upward are not assessed.

Diagonal hatching marks elements outside the scale for that view: no tabulated abundance (abundance), natural traces only (origin), or not assessed (supply risk).

Standard atomic weights use IUPAC/CIAAW abridged and conventional values; a bracketed value is the mass number of the longest-lived isotope. Electron configurations are NIST ground states; configurations for Z ≥ 104 are predicted. Mission facts come from NASA, ESA, JAXA and CNSA mission pages. Page last updated 18 October 2026.
